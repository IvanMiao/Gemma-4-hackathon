## Challenge

Build a useful and original application in one of the three tracks, with Gemma 4 at the core of the solution.

The prototype may use prompting, retrieval-augmented generation, tool use, native function calling, fine-tuning, quantization, or another relevant technique. What matters is that Gemma is essential to the experience and that the team can demonstrate how and why it works.

This hackathon is not about writing polished, production-ready code. It is a sprint to experiment, learn, break things, and build a convincing solution to a real-world problem in just a few hours: team up, brainstorm a bold idea, build a working prototype, and pitch it before the clock runs out. Mentorship and partner credits are available on site.

## Tracks

### Track 1 — Edge / On-Device
Build a mobile, web, desktop, or edge application that runs Gemma locally. Strong entries will make thoughtful use of on-device inference to provide benefits such as privacy, offline access, low latency, efficiency, or accessibility.

### Track 2 — Autonomous Agents
Use Gemma's function-calling and reasoning capabilities to create an agent that can plan, use tools, interact with external APIs, or complete meaningful tasks. The agent should do more than generate text: it should take useful, observable actions.

### Track 3 — Context Engineering for SLMs
Small language models become far more capable when they receive the right context. Design a system that helps Gemma select, structure, compress, retrieve, or update context effectively. Projects may explore memory, RAG, prompt and context optimization, dynamic tool context, agent traces, or other techniques that make a small model more reliable and useful. Supported by Alien Intelligence.

## Goal

Tell a clear story: show the problem, why it matters, and how the Gemma 4 application solves it. Technical execution is vital, but the ability to communicate the vision through a compelling writeup, working demo, and pitch helps set the winning projects apart.

## Submission Requirements

A valid submission must be completed before the Kaggle deadline and include all three items:

1. **A Kaggle Writeup** — the proof of work and technical report. It must:
   - include a title and subtitle;
   - identify one of the three official tracks;
   - explain the problem and proposed solution;
   - describe the project architecture and engineering process;
   - show specifically how Gemma 4 is used;
   - discuss the main challenges encountered during the sprint and the technical choices made; and
   - remain under **1,500 words** (longer submissions may be penalized).
2. **A public code repository** — GitHub or a Kaggle Notebook; accessible without login or paywall, sufficiently documented, and clearly showing the Gemma 4 implementation. Add the link under Attachments → Project Links in the Writeup.
3. **A live demo** — a hosted application, a functional Kaggle Notebook, an interactive terminal recording, or demo files / a short demo video that clearly shows the prototype working. Must be accessible without login or paywall; add its URL or files in the Writeup's Attachments section.

Drafts and unsubmitted Writeups will not be considered. Each team may submit one Writeup and may unsubmit, edit, and resubmit as often as needed before the deadline.

To create a Writeup, click **New Writeup** on the hackathon page: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/paris-gemma-4-hackathon/writeups — once saved, use the **Submit** button in the top-right corner.

Kaggle privacy note: a private Kaggle resource attached to a public Writeup automatically becomes public after the submission deadline.

## Evaluation

Judges do not expect production-ready polish; they focus on core functionality, creative and meaningful use of Gemma, and the quality of the demonstration.

| Criterion | Description | Points |
|---|---|---|
| Gemma Integration | Is Gemma 4 core to the solution? Is it used effectively through prompting, RAG, tool use, fine-tuning, or another relevant technique? | 0–30 |
| Innovation & Impact | Does the application address a meaningful problem? Is the approach creative and relevant? | 0–30 |
| Functionality | Does the prototype work? Is the demo convincing? | 0–20 |
| Presentation & Writeup | Is the project clearly explained? Do the Writeup, demo, and pitch communicate the problem and solution effectively? | 0–20 |
| **Total** | | **100** |

Track awards use this common rubric while taking the track definition into account. The NVIDIA GPU Challenge additionally considers the quality and performance of the NVIDIA-based deployment.

Submissions that are missing a required item, contain an empty or inaccessible repository, or do not meaningfully use a Gemma model may be deemed ineligible before scoring.
