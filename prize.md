Official Competition Rules
Gemma 4 Hackathon | Paris
Entry into this Competition constitutes your acceptance of these Official Competition Rules.

The Gemma 4 Hackathon | Paris (the “Competition”) is a skills-based, one-day, in-person hackathon. You must register through the Competition Website and accept these rules to participate. These Official Competition Rules, the Competition Website, and the applicable Kaggle General Competition Rules together form the “Rules.”

Submitting a project constitutes agreement to the Rules. A Participant or Team that does not comply with the Rules is not eligible to receive a Prize.

You may not enter or submit through multiple Kaggle accounts.

1.5 Tracks
Each Team must select exactly one of the following tracks for its Kaggle Writeup:

Edge / On-Device — a mobile, web, desktop, or edge application that runs Gemma locally and makes meaningful use of benefits such as privacy, offline access, low latency, efficiency, or accessibility.
Autonomous Agents — an application that uses Gemma to plan, use tools, interact with external APIs, or complete meaningful and observable tasks.
Context Engineering for SLMs — a system that improves how Gemma selects, structures, compresses, retrieves, or updates context. Eligible approaches include memory, RAG, prompt and context optimization, dynamic tool context, and agent traces.
Gemma 4 must be central to the submitted solution. Merely mentioning Gemma, calling it in a non-essential part of the project, or using another model as the substantive core of the solution is insufficient.

SerpApi awards

4. Submission Requirements
4.1 Submission limit
Each Team may submit one (1) Kaggle Writeup and select one (1) track. The Team may unsubmit, edit, and resubmit that same Writeup before the Final Submission Deadline.

Drafts and unsubmitted Writeups are not eligible for judging.

4.2 Required deliverables
A valid Submission must include all of the following:

Kaggle Writeup

title and subtitle;
selected track;
problem and proposed solution;
project architecture and engineering process;
a clear explanation of how Gemma 4 is used;
challenges encountered and technical decisions; and
no more than 1,500 words.
Public code repository

accessible without a login or paywall;
sufficiently documented to understand and run the project;
showing the actual Gemma 4 implementation; and
carrying the required open-source licence.
Working demo

a hosted application, functional Kaggle Notebook, interactive terminal recording, demo files, or short demo video;
sufficient for judges to assess the core functionality; and
accessible without a login or paywall.
Links must be placed in the Writeup’s Attachments or Project Links sections, as appropriate.

If a private Kaggle resource is attached to a public Writeup, Kaggle may automatically make it public after the deadline.

4.3 Submission integrity
The Submission must be the Team’s own work. Participants must:

disclose and comply with the licences of reused code, data, models, media, and other third-party materials;
provide attribution where required;
not misrepresent generated, borrowed, or pre-existing work as original work;
not submit malicious code, malware, or content that unlawfully infringes another party’s rights; and
give judges sufficient access to verify material claims made in the Writeup or demo.
The use of AI coding assistants, automated machine-learning tools, external APIs, and pre-existing open-source components is permitted if their use and licences comply with these Rules.

5. Judging
5.1 Eligibility review
Before scoring, the Host may deem a Submission ineligible if it:

is late, remains a draft, or is not submitted;
is missing a required deliverable;
has an empty, private, inaccessible, or materially undocumented repository;
lacks a working or assessable demo;
does not meaningfully use Gemma 4;
exceeds the Writeup limit;
violates a licence, law, Kaggle policy, venue rule, or these Rules; or
cannot be reasonably verified by the judges.
5.2 Evaluation rubric
Eligible Submissions will be evaluated by a human judging panel using the following rubric:

Criterion	Description	Points
Gemma Integration	Is Gemma 4 core to the solution and used effectively through prompting, RAG, tool use, fine-tuning, or another relevant technique?	0–30
Innovation & Impact	Does the application address a meaningful problem? Is the approach creative and relevant?	0–30
Functionality	Does the prototype work? Is the demo convincing?	0–20
Presentation & Writeup	Do the Writeup, demo, and pitch explain the problem and solution clearly and effectively?	0–20
Total	100
Track awards use this common rubric while also taking the selected track definition into account.

The NVIDIA GPU Challenge additionally considers the quality and performance of the NVIDIA-based deployment. SerpApi awards require a substantive and verifiable SerpApi integration.

5.3 Decisions and ties
The judges’ decisions are final. In the event of a tie, the higher Gemma Integration score will prevail, followed by Innovation & Impact, Functionality, and then Presentation & Writeup. If a tie remains, the judging panel will determine the winner by deliberation.

The Host may verify a Submission, request a live demonstration, inspect the public repository, and ask the Team technical questions before confirming an award.
